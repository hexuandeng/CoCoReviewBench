# Abstract

Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGAN) can generate realistic images by incorporating class information into GAN. One of the most popular cGANs is an auxiliary classifier GAN with softmax cross-entropy loss (ACGAN). However, it is widely known that training ACGAN is challenging as the number of classes in the dataset increases. Also, ACGAN tends to generate easily classifiable samples without considering diversity. In this paper, we introduce two cures for ACGAN. First, we identify that gradient exploding in the classifier can cause an undesirable early training collapse, and projecting input vectors onto a unit hypersphere can resolve the problem. Second, we propose Data-to-Data Cross-Entropy loss (D2D-CE) to exploit fruitful relational information in the class-labeled dataset. On this foundation, we propose the Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Network (ReACGAN). The experimental results show that ReACGAN achieves state-of-the-art generation performances on CIFAR10, CUB200, Tiny-Imagenet, andImagenet datasets. In addition, we show that ReACGAN can benefit from consistency regularization and differentiable augmentations for better performance. All weights, logs, and source codes will be released to the public.

# 1 Introduction

Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) [1] are known for the forefront approach for generating high-fidelity images of diverse categories [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. Behind the sensational generation ability of GANs, there has been tremendous effort to develop adversarial objectives free from vanishing gradient problem [10, 11, 12], regularizations for stabilizing adversarial training [11, 13, 14, 3, 15, 16, 17], and conditioning techniques to support the adversarial training using category information of dataset [18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25]. Subsequently, the conditioning techniques have become the de facto standard for high-quality image generation. The models with the conditioning methods are called Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGAN), and cGANs can be divided into two groups depending on the discriminator's conditioning way: classifier-based GANs [18, 20, 22, 23, 24, 25] and projection-based GANs [3, 4, 19].

The classifier-based GANs facilitate an auxiliary classifier to generate class-specific images by penalizing the generator if the synthesized outputs are not consistent with the conditioned labels. ACGAN [18] has been one of the widely used classifier-based GANs for their simple design and satisfactory generation performance. While ACGAN can exploit class information by pushing and pulling classifier's weights (proxy) against image embeddings [22, 24], it is well known that ACGAN training is prone to collapse at the early stage of training as the number of classes increases [19, 22, 24]. Also, the generator of ACGAN tends to generate easily classifiable images at the cost of reduced diversity [18, 19]. Projection-based GANs, on the other hand, have shown cutting-edge generation results on datasets with a large number of categories. SNGAN [3], SAGAN [26], and BigGAN [4]

However, projection-based GANs only consider a pairwise relationship between an image and its label proxy (data-to-class relationships). As the result, the projection-based GANs can miss an additional opportunity to consider relation information between data instances (data-to-data relationships) and between instance categories (class-to-class relationships) as discovered by [22, 24].  
In this paper, we analyze why ACGAN training becomes unstable as the number of classes increases and then propose remedies for (1) the instability and (2) the relatively poor generation performance of ACGAN compared to the projection-based models. First, we begin by analytically calculating gradients of the softmax cross-entropy loss used in ACGAN. By examining the exact values of analytic gradients, we discover that the unboundedness of input feature vectors and poor classification performance in the early training phase can cause an undesirable gradient exploding problem. Second, with alleviating the instability, we propose the Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Networks (ReACGAN) using the Data-to-data Cross-Entropy loss (D2D-CE). ReACGAN projects image embeddings and proxies onto a unit hypersphere and computes similarities for data-to-data and data-to-class consideration. Additionally, we introduce two threshold values for inter-class and intra-class separability. In this way, ReACGAN overcomes the training instability and can exploit fruitful supervisory signals by explicitly considering data-to-class and data-to-data relationships, and also by implicitly looking at class-to-class relationships in the same mini-batch.  
To validate our approach, we conduct image generation experiments using four datasets: CIFAR10 [27], CUB200 [29], Tiny-Imagenet [30], andImagenet [28] using various evaluation metrics: Inception Score (IS) [31], Frechet Inception Distance (FID) [32], and Precision and Recall [33]. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that ReACGAN beats both classifier- and projection-based cGANs and achieves state-of-the-art results by  $2.5\%$ ,  $15.8\%$ ,  $5.1\%$ , and  $20.2\%$  in terms of FID on the four datasets, respectively. In addition, we confirm that ReACGAN is well harmonized with consistency regularizations [16] and differentiable augmentations [9]. In the ablation study, we verify the effectiveness of each part of ReACGAN.

# 2 Background: Generative Adversarial Networks

Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) [1] is an implicit generative model that aims to generate a sample indistinguishable from the real. GAN consists of two networks: a Generator  $G:\mathcal{Z}\longrightarrow \mathcal{X}$  that tries to map a latent variable  $\mathbf{z}\sim p(\mathbf{z})$  into the real data space  $\mathcal{X}$  and a Discriminator  $D:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow$

[0, 1] that strives to discriminate whether a given sample  $\mathbf{x}$  is from the real data distribution  $p_{\mathrm{real}}(\mathbf{x})$  or the implicit distribution  $p_{\mathrm{gen}}(G(\mathbf{z}))$  derived from the generator  $G(\mathbf{z})$ . The objective of a vanilla GAN [1] can be expressed as follows:

$$
\min  _ {G} \max  _ {D} \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {x} \sim p (\mathbf {x})} [ \log (D (\mathbf {x})) ] + \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {z} \sim p (\mathbf {z})} [ \log (1 - D (G (\mathbf {z}))) ]. \tag {1}
$$

While GANs have shown impressive outputs in the image generation task [2, 34, 11, 13], training GANs often ends up encountering a mode-collapse problem [35, 11, 12]. As one of the prescriptions for stabilizing and reinforcing GANs, training GANs with categorical information, named Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGAN), is suggested [36, 18, 19]. Depending on the presence of explicit classification losses, cGAN can be divided into two groups: Classifier-based GANs [18, 20, 25, 22, 23, 24] and Projection-based GANs [19, 3, 4]. ACGAN is one of the widely used classifier-based GANs with softmax cross-entropy loss. ACGAN utilizes an auxiliary classifier and is trained to perform adversarial training and image classification jointly. Although ACGAN has shown satisfactory generation results, training ACGAN becomes unstable as the number of classes in the training dataset increases [19, 22, 24]. Besides, ACGAN tends to generate easily classifiable images at the cost of limited diversity. To alleviate those problems, Zhou et al. [37] propose performing adversarial training on the classifier. Gong et al. [20] deploy one more classifier to train a missing entropy objective in ACGAN. However, ACGAN training still suffers from the early training collapse and reduced diversity problems when training ACGAN models on datasets with the large number of class categories, such as Tiny-Imagenet [30], andImagenet [28].

In this situation, Miyato et al. [19] propose a projection discriminator for cGAN and show significant improvement in generating Imagenet dataset with a single pair of the generator and discriminator. Motivated by the promising generation results, many projection-based models [3, 26, 4, 16, 6, 17] are proposed and become the standard for conditional generation. In this paper, we revisit ACGAN and try to unveil the source of instability. Coping with the instability, we propose the Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier GANs for high-quality and diverse image generation with stable training.

# 3 Rebooting Auxiliary Classifier GANs

# 3.1 Feature Normalization

To uncover a nuisance that causes the instability of ACGAN, we start by analytically calculating gradients of weight vectors in the softmax classifier. Let the part of the discriminator before the fully connected layer be a Feature extractor  $F: \mathcal{X} \longrightarrow \mathcal{F} \in \mathbb{R}^d$  and let Classifier  $C: \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^c$  be a single fully connected layer parameterized by  $W = [\pmb{w}_1, \dots, \pmb{w}_c] \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times c}$ , where  $c$  denotes the number of classess. We can sample training images  $X = \{\pmb{x}_1, \dots, \pmb{x}_N\}$  and integer labels  $y = \{y_1, \dots, y_N\}$  from the joint distribution  $p(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$ . Using above notations, we can express the empirical cross-entropy loss used in ACGAN [18] as follows:

$$
\mathcal {L} _ {\mathrm {C E}} = - \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \log \left(\frac {\exp \left(F \left(\boldsymbol {x} _ {i}\right) ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {w} _ {y _ {i}}\right)}{\sum_ {j = 1} ^ {c} \exp \left(F \left(\boldsymbol {x} _ {i}\right) ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {w} _ {y _ {j}}\right)}\right). \tag {3}
$$

Based on this, we can calculate the derivative of the cross-entropy loss, w.r.t  $\pmb{w}_{k\in \{1,\dots,c\}}$  as follows:

$$
\frac {\partial \mathcal {L} _ {\mathrm {C E}}}{\partial \boldsymbol {w} _ {k}} = - \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \left\{F \left(\boldsymbol {x} _ {i}\right) \left(\mathbf {1} _ {y _ {i} = k} - p _ {i, k}\right) \right\}, \tag {4}
$$

where  $\mathbf{1}_{y_i = k}$  is an indicator function that will output 1 when  $y_{i} = k$  is satisfied, and  $p_{i,k}$  is a class probability that represents the probability of  $i$ -th sample belongs to class  $k$ , mathematically  $\frac{\exp(F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)^\top\boldsymbol{w}_k)}{\sum_{j = 1}^c\exp(F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)^\top\boldsymbol{w}_j)}$ . The above equation implies that the norm of gradient of the softmax cross-entropy loss is closely related to the norms and directions of each input feature map  $F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)$  and the class probability  $p_{i,k}$ . In the early training stage, the classifier is prone to make incorrect predictions, resulting in low probability outputs. This phenomenon gets severe as the number of categories of the dataset increases. As a result, the norm of the gradient  $\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{w}_k}\right|$  begins to explode as the inner-product value of  $F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)^\top \boldsymbol{w}_k$  arises, and the vector  $F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)$  stretches out to  $\boldsymbol{w}_k$  direction. This may break the balance between adversarial and classification training and can lead to early training collapse. In

As one of the remedies for the gradient exploding problem, we find that simply normalizing the feature embeddings onto a unit hypersphere  $\frac{F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)}{||F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)||}$  effectively resolves ACGAN's early training collapse (see Fig. 2). The motivation is that normalizing features onto the hypersphere will restrict the norm of gradient under 1.0 explicitly. Thus the discriminator does not experience the gradient exploding problem. From the next section, we will normalize both feature embeddings and weight vectors (proxies) together because applying normalization on both features and weight vectors does not degrade image generation performance, and it is convenient for future development. We denote the normalized feature  $\frac{F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)}{||F(\boldsymbol{x}_i)||}$  as  $\pmb{f}_i$  and the normalized weight vector  $\frac{\pmb{w}_{y_i}}{||\pmb{w}_{y_i}||}$  as  $\pmb{v}_{y_i}$ .

# 3.2 Data-to-Data Cross-Entropy loss (D2D-CE)

We expand the feature normalized softmax cross-entropy loss described in Sec. 3.1 to Data-to-Data Cross-Entropy (D2D-CE) loss in this section. The motivations can be summarized in two points: (1) replace data-to-class similarities in the denominator of Eq. (3) with data-to-data similarities and (2) introduce strict thresholds to the modified softmax cross-entropy loss. We expect that point (1) will encourage the feature extractor to consider data-to-data as well as data-to-class relationships. Also, point (2) will guarantee inter-class separability and intra-class variations in the feature space while preventing ineffective gradient updates induced by easy negative and positive samples. To develop the feature normalized cross-entropy loss into D2D-CE, we firstly replace the similarities between a sample embedding and all label proxies except for the positive one in the denominator,  $\sum_{j\in \{1,\dots ,c\} \setminus \{y_i\}}\exp (f_i^\top v_j)$  with similarities between negative samples in the same mini-batch. The modified cross-entropy loss can be expressed as follows:

$$
\mathcal {L} _ {\mathrm {C E}} ^ {\prime} = - \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \log \left(\frac {\exp \left(\boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {v} _ {y _ {i}} / \tau\right)}{\exp \left(\boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {v} _ {y _ {i}} / \tau\right) + \sum_ {j \in \mathcal {N} (i)} \exp \left(\boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {f} _ {j} / \tau\right)}\right), \tag {5}
$$

where  $\tau$  is a temperature hyperparameter, and  $\mathcal{N}(i)$  is the set of indices that indicate negative samples whose labels are different from the reference embedding  $f_{i}$  in the mini-batch. This process is described in Fig 3. The similarity matrix calculated by two mini-batches of sample embeddings is used to calculate the similarities between negative samples in the denominator with False Negative Mask. As a result, Eq. (5) encourages the discriminator to compare differences between data instances, so it will give a rich supervisory signal for conditional image generation. Finally, we introduce threshold values to  $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\prime}$  and name it Data-to-Data Cross-Entropy loss (D2D-CE). The proposed D2D-CE can be expressed as follows:

$$
\mathcal {L} _ {\mathrm {D 2 D - C E}} = - \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \log \left(\frac {\exp \left([ \boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {v} _ {y _ {i}} - m _ {p} ] _ {-} / \tau\right)}{\exp \left([ \boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {v} _ {y _ {i}} - m _ {p} ] _ {-} / \tau\right) + \sum_ {j \in \mathcal {N} (i)} \exp \left([ \boldsymbol {f} _ {i} ^ {\top} \boldsymbol {f} _ {j} - m _ {n} ] _ {+} / \tau\right)}\right), \tag {6}
$$

# 3.3 Useful Properties of Data-to-Data Cross-Entropy loss

In this subsection, we explain four useful properties of D2D-CE. Let  $s_i$  be a similarity between the reference sample  $\pmb{f}_i$  and the corresponding normalized proxy  $\pmb{v}_{y_i}$ ,  $s_{i,j}$  be a similarity between  $\pmb{f}_i$  and  $\pmb{f}_j$ , and  $a,b$  are arbitrary indices of negative samples, i.e.,  $a,b\in \mathcal{N}(i)$ . Then the properties of D2D-CE loss can be summarized as follows:

Property 1. Hard Negative Mining. If the value of  $\pmb{s}_{i,a}$  is greater than  $\pmb{s}_{i,b}$ , the derivative of  $\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}$  w.r.t  $\pmb{s}_{i,a}$  is greater than or equal to the derivative w.r.t  $\pmb{s}_{i,b}$ ; that is  $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}}{\partial\pmb{s}_{i,a}}\geq \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}}{\partial\pmb{s}_{i,b}}\geq 0$ .

Property 2. Positive threshold. If  $\pmb{s}_i - m_p > 0$ , the derivative of  $\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}$  w.r.t  $\pmb{s}_i$  is 0.

Property 3. Negative threshold. If  $s_{i,j} - m_n < 0$ , the derivative of  $\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}$  w.r.t  $s_{i,j}$  is 0.

Property 4.  $\mathcal{L}_{D2D-CE}$  has the global minima of  $\log (1 + |\mathcal{N}(i)|)$  when there does not exist an arbitrary  $i$  and  $j$  such that  $s_i - m_p < 0$  or  $s_{i,j} - m_n > 0$ .

We put proofs of the above properties in the supplement. Property 1 shows that D2D-CE can implicitly conduct hard negative mining and benefit from comparing samples each other. Also, Property 2 and 3 implies that samples will not affect gradient updates if the sample are trained sufficiently. As a result, the classifier can concentrate on pushing and pulling hard negative and positive examples without being dominated by easy negative and positive samples.

# 3.4 Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Networks (ReACGAN)

With the proposed D2D-CE objective, we propose the Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Networks (ReACGAN). As ACGAN does, ReACGAN jointly optimizes adversarial and

classification objectives. Specifically, the discriminator of ReACGAN tries to discriminate whether a given image is sampled from the real distribution or not. At the same time, the discriminator tries to maximize similarities between real positive samples while minimizing similarities between real negative samples using D2D-CE loss. After predetermined numbers of the discriminator updates end, the generator strives to deceive the discriminator by generating realistic images that will give a low D2D-CE objective value. By alternatively training the discriminator and generator until convergence, ReACGAN can generate high-quality images of diverse categories without early training collapse. Differences between ReACGAN and ContraGAN. The authors of ContraGAN [22] propose a conditional contrastive loss (2C loss) to cover data-to-data relationships when training cGANs. The main differences between 2C loss and D2D-CE are summed up into three points: (1) while 2C loss is derived from NT-Xent loss [38], which is popularly used in self-supervised learning, our D2D-CE is developed to resolve the early training collapsing problem and poor generation performance of ACGAN, (2) 2C loss holds false negative samples in the denominator, and it causes unexpected forces to repulse the positive samples out of the reference image, and (3) 2C loss contains similarities between all positive samples in the numerator, and it will give rise to large gradients on easy positive samples. As a result, GANs with 2C loss tend to synthesize images of unintended class as reported in the original author's implementation [39]. However, D2D-CE loss does not contain the false negatives in the denominator and only considers the similarity between data and its proxy in the numerator. Therefore, ReACGAN does not conduct unnecessary easy positive mining and is free from the unwanted repelling forces. More detailed explanations of this are attached in the supplement.

Consistency Regularization and D2D-CE loss. Zhang et al. [16] propose consistency regularization to let the discriminator output consistent predictions if given two images are visually close to each other. They make a visually similar image pair by augmenting the reference image with pre-defined augmentations. After that, they force the discriminator to minimize the L2 distance between logits of the reference and augmented images. While ReACGAN locates an image embedding nearby to its corresponding proxy and far apart from multiple image embeddings according to class labels, consistency regularization only pulls the original and augmented images towards each other. Since Consistency Regularization and D2D-CE can be applied together, we will show that ReACGAN benefits from consistency regularization in the experiments section (Table 1, 2).

# 4 Experiments

# 4.1 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics

To verify the effectiveness of ReACGAN, we conduct conditional image generation experiments using four datasets: CIFAR10 [27], Tiny-Imagenet [30], CUB200 [29], andImagenet [28] and four evaluation metrics: Inception Score (IS) [31], Frechet Inception Distance (FID) [32], and  $\mathrm{F_{0.125}}$  (Precision) and  $\mathrm{F_8}$  (Recall) [33]. The details of training datasets are in the supplement.

Inception Score (IS) [31] and Frechet Inception Distance (FID) [32] are commonly used metrics for generative model evaluation. We utilize IS and FID together because some studies [4, 6, 24] have shown that IS has a tendency to indicate the fidelity of images better while FID tends to capture the diversity of samples.

Precision  $(\mathbf{F}_{0.125})$  and Recall  $(\mathbf{F}_8)$  [33] are metrics for estimating precision and recall of the approximated distribution  $p(G(\mathbf{z}))$  against the true data distribution  $p(\mathbf{x})$ . Instead of evaluating generative models using one-dimensional scores, such as IS and FID, Sajjadi et al. [33] suggest using two-dimensional score  $\mathrm{F_{0.125}}$  and  $\mathrm{F_8}$  that can quantify how precisely the generated images are and how well the generated images cover the reference distribution.

# 284 5 Conclusion

In this paper, we have analyzed why training ACGAN becomes unstable as the number of classes of the dataset increases. By deriving the analytic form of gradients of the classifier and numerically computing the gradient values, we have discovered that the training instability comes from a gradient exploding problem caused by the unboundedness of input feature vectors and poor classification ability in the early training phase. To alleviate the instability and reinforce ACGAN models, we have proposed a novel Data-to-Data cross-entropy loss (D2D-CE) and Rebooted Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Networks (ReACGAN). Although ReACGAN requires additional hyperparameters of temperature and threshold values, we have found that they do not significantly affect generation performance. The experimental results demonstrate the superiority of ReACGAN compared with the existing classifier and projection-based GANs on four benchmark datasets. We would like to conduct a large-scale conditional image generation experiment onImagenet with a larger batch size as future work. Also, we want to explore applying more diverse augmentations used in SimCLR [38], adaptive discriminator augmentations [8], and contrastive discriminator [44] to identify that ReACGAN can benefit from more complex data augmentations.
